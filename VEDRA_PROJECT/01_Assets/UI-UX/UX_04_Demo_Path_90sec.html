Correcting my own flag

I said "open on Mission Control." That was wrong.

Opening on density is the trap every AI product falls into — look how much it does! Strangers don't buy activity. They buy a specific outcome they recognise as their own pain, and then they buy the belief that it compounds.

The sparse first screen isn't the problem. Showing it before it's been earned is. So don't skip it — end on it. The emptiness is the punchline, not the opening.

The structural problem, stated properly. Kalpavriksha's value is a derivative — it lives in the change between day 1 and day 200. You cannot demo a derivative with a screenshot. So the demo isn't a tour of screens. It's a time-lapse of a relationship.

Arc: noise → judgment → compounding → silence. Four beats, one emotional shape: this is my life → it did something I'd pay a person for → it gets better on its own → and then it leaves me alone.

Non-negotiables

Six rules, and the reason for each.

These are what make the 90 seconds work. Break any one of them and the demo becomes a feature tour, which is the genre this product is trying to escape.

Rule 01

Open on their pain, not your product

The first twelve seconds contain no interface at all. Two numbers on black: 340 approvals, 312 of them under four minutes. The prospect has to recognise themselves before they can want the cure. If they don't nod here, nothing after it lands.

Rule 02

Hand them the mouse at beat 3

They click Send the counter themselves. Not you. A demo watched is a demo forgotten; a decision made with your own hand is a commitment, however small. It is also the only moment in 90 seconds where they experience being the principal rather than the audience.

Rule 03

Answer "what if it's wrong?" before they ask

At beat 4, four small words sit under the rule proposal: undo 24h · cap ₹2L · expires 30 days · pause one click. Four seconds of screen time. Every autonomy objection is pre-empted while they're still excited, never after they've gone cold.

Rule 04

One chart, and it's a slope

The autonomy ratio climbing from 18% to 91% is the only chart in the demo. Not usage, not tasks, not time saved. A rising slope is the only visual that can express a derivative — and the derivative is the product.

Rule 05

The tree appears last, or not at all

Every AI startup opens on a glowing particle animation. Leading with it files you in that drawer within two seconds. Here it's atmosphere behind the final frame — the reward for having earned the silence, not the hook.

Rule 06

Never demo live

An AI executive improvising in front of a prospect is a coin flip on your credibility. Run a deterministic demo tenant with real data shape and fixed outcomes. Say so openly — "this is a scripted tenant" buys more trust than a live run that stumbles.

Cut from the 90

What doesn't make the reel

✕
Conversation mode. Painful cut — it's lovely. But every AI product on earth demos a chat box. It is the single least differentiated thing you own, and in 90 seconds it costs you the beat that isn't. Save it for minute six.
✕
Mission Control. Density reads as complexity to a stranger. It's the right screen for the technical evaluator in the 15-minute call, and the wrong one for the first impression.
✕
The integrations wall. A grid of logos says "another tool to configure." Integrations are a procurement answer, not a value answer.
✕
Voice. It delights in person and it dies on a shared screen with bad audio. Optional flourish for in-room demos only; never in the async version.
✕
Any sentence beginning "and it can also…" The 90 seconds proves one claim: it does real work and asks for you less over time. A second claim halves the first.
Variants off the same spine

Three lengths, one arc

90 SECONDS · COLD

All six beats as built. Conference booth, cold intro, the first ninety seconds of any call. Ends on silence and one line: "You're not buying a dashboard. You're buying that."

3 MINUTES · ASYNC VIDEO

Same six beats, plus the flagged judgment call from the receipt — "I approved this under your rule but the pattern might not be what you meant." It needs a beat of its own to land, and async viewers will rewind it. That clip is the one that gets forwarded.

15 MINUTES · QUALIFIED

Run the 90 first, unchanged, then open the floor. Now Mission Control, the queue at volume, conversation mode, and the rule library all earn their place — because the buyer is asking, not being shown.

The closing line

"You're not buying a dashboard. You're buying that."

Said over the silent frame, after a four-second hold. Then stop talking. The instinct will be to fill the pause with features — the pause is the product.
