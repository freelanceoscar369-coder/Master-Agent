SCREEN 01 · V2 Why the first screen looks like this

The dashboard is now a footnote to a sentence.

If the AI is the interface, then the first screen is not a place where information is displayed. It is a moment where an intelligence reports to a principal. Everything that isn't the report or the decision had to go — including most of what I designed in v1.

The test I applied to every element. Does this explain what the AI did, or does it ask for human judgment? If neither, it was deleted. Not moved, not collapsed — deleted.

What I killed from my own v1

Four deletions

✕
The stat row. "12 completed / 3 improved / 2 recommendations / 1 approval." Four big numerals I was proud of. But "12 completed" doesn't explain what was done and doesn't require judgment — it's vanity telemetry dressed as mission control. It became one line: 12 handled without you.
✕
The greeting as its own beat. "Good evening, Onkar." full stop, was the most valuable moment in the product spent on nothing. The greeting now carries payload in the same breath: "Two things need you. This one first — it expires Friday."
✕
Scroll. The v1 first screen was ceremony, then you scrolled into a dashboard. If the first screen scrolls, it's a dashboard again. This one fits a viewport and empties.
✕
Navigation. There is no rail, no menu, no tabs. Three lowercase doorways sit at the bottom for when the founder doubts. You go to Mission Control when you don't trust the summary — so its prominence should be inversely proportional to how well the AI is doing its job.
What replaced them

Three elements. That's the whole screen.

01 · The voice
The AI speaks first, in prose, at 38px. Not a card. Not a widget. A sentence with a subject and a verb.
Why
Prose is the only format that can carry judgment. A metric can't say "I'd open at ₹13.8L."
02 · The decision
One decision, in full, with its recommendation and its consequence. Not a link to an approval centre.
Why
Where judgment is required is the product. It gets the largest object on screen.
03 · The receipt
Everything done, collapsed to two numbers, expandable. Deliberately low-contrast.
Why
Explaining what it did is an obligation, not a boast. It should be available, not loud.
Decision 01

One decision at a time, never a list

A chief of staff doesn't hand you a stack. They say "this one first, here's why." The consequence ranking from Screen 04b already produces the order — the first screen just takes the top item and shows it completely. Resolve it and the next one rises into the same slot. The screen empties as you work. Try it.

Decision 02

The AI reports its own judgment calls

Inside the receipt, one item is flagged: "I approved this under your rule, but the pattern might not be what you meant. Want me to narrow it?" Nothing else in this product does more for trust. An AI that only reports successes is a marketing surface; one that surfaces its own borderline calls — unprompted, when nobody would have caught it — is a colleague.

Decision 03

The tree is the emotional register

It's no longer a ceremony that ends. It lives behind the words for the entire session, weighted left, dimmed under the text. It tightens and warms toward amber when judgment is pending, and settles into a slow cool breathe when the screen is clear. The founder reads the system's state before reading a word.

The success condition

This screen should get emptier every month.

Same inversion as the approval queue. As standing rules accumulate, the number on the left shrinks and the number on the right grows. Eventually the founder opens Kalpavriksha and reads: "Nothing needs you. Here's what I'm doing." — with three lines of receipt beneath it. That is not a degraded state of the product. That is the product having won.

Switch the demo control to All clear to see it.

The risk I'm accepting, stated openly. A screen this sparse can read as "the product doesn't do much" in a demo to someone who hasn't lived with it. Density signals value to a stranger; restraint signals value to an owner. I'm optimising for the owner on day 200, not the stranger on day 0 — but that means the sales demo needs Mission Control, not this screen. Worth deciding deliberately rather than discovering later.
